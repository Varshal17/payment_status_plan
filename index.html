      Part 1

      Content design system

      Customers should always know three things about a payment: where it is, when it will finish, and whether they need to do anything. This system gives every payment product the same seven states, the same words and the same building blocks to answer those questions. It works on any surface, from an app chip to an AI agent.

      It has four layers, and each has an owner:

      Layer What it defines Owner
      Words Labels, vocabulary, message patterns, templates Content design
      Design Status chip, tracker, timeline, banner, notification; colour and icon tokens Product design
      Tech Status event, content IDs, fallback rules, rail mapping registry Engineering
      Product Time promises, allowed actions, notification triggers, regulatory hooks Product management

      How it scales: a new payment method or rail never gets new copy by default. It gets mapping rows that point its raw statuses at existing states and causes. The words, design and actions follow automatically.

      Evidence base: the companion Payment Status Benchmark compares 24 banks, fintechs, payment rails and standards on how they structure and word status. Decisions here cite it as "the benchmark." The whiteboard shows the cross-surface architecture.

      Principles

      Each principle names the rule that enforces it. A principle with no rule is decoration.

      # Principle What it means Enforced by
      P1 Few states, many causes Seven states cover every payment. Detail goes in the cause line, never in a new state. LINT-STATE-01: label must come from the state registry. New state needs a CDR.
      P2 Where the money is Every not-done message says where the money is right now. LINT-MONEY-01: NOT_COMPLETED and CHANGED_AFTER_DONE templates must say where the money is (money_moved).
      P3 Ranges, not promises Give an expected-by range or a latest date. Never "instant." LINT-TIME-01: bans instant, immediately, real-time, soon, shortly.
      P4 Name who acts Say who is doing what: us, their bank, the network, you. LINT-AGENT-01: bans agentless passive ("could not be processed").
      P5 One next step Every message ends with one action, or says plainly that nothing is needed. LINT-NEXT-01: next_step slot is required on every template.
      P6 Same words everywhere App, email, SMS, agent and statement use one label per state. LINT-LEX-01: lexicon never terms block; surface copy resolves from one content ID.
      P7 Never colour alone State shows as label + icon + colour. Colour is a hint. DS-A11Y-01: chip component requires label and icon props.
      P8 Rails map in, words don't change A new rail adds mapping rows, not copy. GOV-RAIL-01: a rail PR with new content IDs fails unless a CDR is attached.
      P9 The agent says what the app says AI answers are grounded in the same state and content. AGT-01: agent reads get_payment_status; agent_must_not_say list is checked on output.

      The status set

      Seven states. Every payment, on every rail, is in exactly one of them at a time. Customers see the label. Systems see the state ID.

      State ID Customer label Meaning (one test) Situation Colour role Terminal?
      SCHEDULED Scheduled Set up for a future date. No money has moved. Routine Neutral No
      ACTION_NEEDED Action needed It cannot move until the customer does something. Blocked Warning No
      IN_PROGRESS In progress Money has left or is held, and is not yet where it is going. Waiting Info No
      COMPLETED Completed The money reached its destination. Routine / Celebratory Success Yes, until changed
      AVAILABLE Available Received money is ready to spend or withdraw. Routine Success Yes
      NOT_COMPLETED Failed · Declined · Canceled · Expired It stopped. Label depends on who stopped it. Failure Critical (Canceled: Neutral) Yes
      CHANGED_AFTER_DONE Returned · Refunded · Disputed Something happened after it completed. Failure / Routine Warning Yes (Disputed resolves to Refunded or back to COMPLETED)

      Why seven

      • The benchmark found about 30 words for these states. "Pending" alone covers 5+ situations. Seven states is the smallest set that still answers "do I need to act?" and "is my money safe?"
      • ACTION_NEEDED is split out because no market label for it exists today. It is the one state where silence costs the customer money.
      • AVAILABLE is split from COMPLETED only for receiving journeys (incoming transfers, refunds to balance, UPI credits). For outgoing payments, COMPLETED is the end. Products that never hold incoming funds skip AVAILABLE.
      • NOT_COMPLETED has one state but four labels. The label tells the customer who stopped it. The state stays one so logic and analytics stay simple.

      NOT_COMPLETED labels

      Label Use when Example
      Declined The receiving side or network refused it Card issuer refused, beneficiary bank rejected
      Failed It broke in transit; nobody chose to stop it Timeout, technical error, rail outage
      Canceled The customer or we stopped it on purpose Customer canceled a scheduled payment
      Expired A time window passed with no action Payment request or mandate not approved in time

      CHANGED_AFTER_DONE labels

      Label Use when
      Returned The receiving bank sent the money back (ACH return, bounced bill payment)
      Refunded The merchant or payee gave money back, or a dispute was decided in the customer's favour
      Disputed The customer opened a dispute and it is still open. Cause: dispute_review.

      The lifecycle

      The diagram below shows the allowed moves. Any move not drawn is a bug, and the event pipeline rejects it.

      Allowed transitions: SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS, ACTION_NEEDED, NOT_COMPLETED (Canceled). ACTION_NEEDED → IN_PROGRESS, NOT_COMPLETED (Expired or Canceled). IN_PROGRESS → ACTION_NEEDED, COMPLETED, NOT_COMPLETED. NOT_COMPLETED → COMPLETED (rare late approval, e.g. Razorpay late authorisation). COMPLETED → AVAILABLE, CHANGED_AFTER_DONE. CHANGED_AFTER_DONE (Disputed) → CHANGED_AFTER_DONE (Refunded) if the customer wins, or back to COMPLETED if they don't (CDR-0004).

      Causes and modifiers

      The state says where a payment is. Causes and modifiers say why, and what to show. They change the headline and body, never the label.

      Causes

      A cause is one value from a closed list. Each state allows only some causes.

      Cause Allowed in Customer meaning Example headline
      processing IN_PROGRESS Normal handling "Your payment is on its way to {payee}"
      bank_review IN_PROGRESS Our or their bank is checking it "We're checking this payment"
      hold IN_PROGRESS Funds held, final amount may change (card auth, tip) "{merchant} is holding {amount}"
      waiting_on_other_party IN_PROGRESS It left us; the receiving bank, biller or network has it "{party} has your payment"
      waiting_on_recipient IN_PROGRESS The person being paid must claim or enrol "Waiting for {payee} to accept"
      verify_identity ACTION_NEEDED We need proof it's them "Confirm it's you to send this payment"
      approve_request ACTION_NEEDED A request or mandate needs approval "{payee} asked you for {amount}"
      add_funds ACTION_NEEDED Not enough balance to run a scheduled payment "Add money to pay {payee} on {date}"
      fix_details ACTION_NEEDED Account or payee details need fixing "Check {payee}'s details"
      insufficient_funds NOT_COMPLETED, CHANGED_AFTER_DONE Not enough money "Not enough money to pay {payee}"
      details_wrong NOT_COMPLETED, CHANGED_AFTER_DONE Receiving account not found or closed "{party} couldn't find this account"
      limit_reached NOT_COMPLETED Daily or per-payment limit "This is over your {limit_type} limit"
      risk_block NOT_COMPLETED Stopped for security "We stopped this payment to protect your account"
      technical NOT_COMPLETED System or network failure "This payment didn't go through"
      customer_canceled NOT_COMPLETED Customer stopped it "You canceled this payment"
      hold_released NOT_COMPLETED Merchant never collected a card hold "{merchant} released the hold"
      time_expired NOT_COMPLETED Window passed "This request expired"
      merchant_refund CHANGED_AFTER_DONE Merchant sent money back "{merchant} refunded {amount}"
      bank_return CHANGED_AFTER_DONE Receiving bank sent it back "{party} sent your payment back"
      dispute_review CHANGED_AFTER_DONE A dispute is open "We're looking into your dispute"

      Rule: if no cause fits, use the state's default cause (processing or technical) and log cause_unmapped. Never invent a cause in copy.

      Modifiers

      Modifiers are flags and values that shape the message. They are optional unless marked required.

      Modifier Type What it changes Required?
      is_delayed boolean Adds "This is taking longer than usual" line; promotes notification No
      eta date range Fills "Expected by {eta}" Required for IN_PROGRESS if the rail gives one
      max_by date Fills "No later than {max_by}" when only a ceiling is known (RBI TAT, Reg E, Reg Z) No
      money_moved enum: not_taken, held, taken, returned Fills the "where your money is" line Required for NOT_COMPLETED and CHANGED_AFTER_DONE
      money_location enum: your_account, with_us, with_other_party, with_payee Same line, finer detail Required with money_moved: taken
      tracking_available boolean Shows or hides the tracker steps. False on SWIFT without tracking, some billers No
      our_status / their_status state ID Shows split view ("Sent by us · Not yet received") No
      final_amount money Timeline line when a held amount changes No
      amount_received money Shows what arrived when intermediaries took fees No
      fee_applied money States a returned-payment or late fee Required if a fee was charged
      provisional_credit money States temporary credit during a dispute Required if credit was given
      reason_code string (rail native) Never shown. Logged and passed to agents and support Yes, when the rail gives one
      allowed_actions list Drives buttons: cancel, edit, retry, dispute, contact Required
      journey enum: pay_bill, send, receive, card_purchase, refund, request Picks the milestone word and template Required

      Milestone words

      COMPLETED keeps one label. The headline names the milestone by journey, so the label stays steady and the meaning stays clear.

      Journey Headline verb
      pay_bill "{payee} received your payment"
      send "{payee} received {amount}"
      receive "You received {amount} from {payer}"
      card_purchase "Paid to {merchant}"
      refund "Refund of {amount} completed"
      request "{payer} paid your request"

      Vocabulary

      One concept, one word. Never terms block a release. Avoid terms warn.

      Status words

      Concept Use Avoid Never Why
      Future-dated Scheduled Upcoming, Planned, Queued — Bank of America and Apple Card both use "Scheduled" (benchmark Part 1).
      Customer must act Action needed Attention required, Incomplete Pending No market word exists. Stripe shows three different states as "Incomplete"; "Pending" hides that the customer is the blocker.
      Moving In progress Processing, Pending, Initiated Submitted (as a label) "Pending" covers card holds, waiting on recipient and compliance review in the benchmark. HSBC's tracker uses "In progress."
      Reached destination Completed Success, Successful, Done, Posted, Settled Approved (for outgoing) Revolut, PayPal, HSBC and Jovia/Zelle use "Completed." "Posted" and "Settled" are ledger words. NPCI uses both "Approved" and "Success" for one state.
      Ready to use Available Cleared, Credited — Says what the customer can do. Plaid's funds_available separates it from settled.
      Refused by other side Declined Rejected, Denied Invalid Card-network norm (Monzo). "Invalid" judges the person.
      Broke in transit Failed Unsuccessful, Error Something went wrong Used by Google Pay, PhonePe, Cash App. Short and honest.
      Stopped on purpose Canceled Voided, Stopped, Reverted — One spelling per locale, set in meta.locale (open decision 1).
      Window passed Expired Timed out, Lapsed — Used by Jovia/Zelle, Shopify, RTP request for payment.
      Sent back by bank Returned Bounced, Bounced back, Reversed NSF Plaid and PayPal use "Returned" (with different meanings; our definition is fixed above).
      Merchant gave back Refunded Credited back, Reversed — Used by Stripe, Shopify, PayPal, Wise.
      Customer contested Disputed Claim, Chargeback — "Chargeback" is a network term. Use in agent definitions only.

      Journey words

      Concept Use Avoid Never Why
      The act Pay, Transfer, Send Initiate, Submit, Process, Set up a payment Execute UK Open Banking terminology guidance: use Pay, Transfer, To; avoid "payment initiation" and "set up a payment."
      Recipient {name} / "who you're paying" Recipient Payee, Beneficiary Open Banking guidance avoids "payee." Use the name.
      Following a payment Track Trace, Monitor — HSBC, Swift and Wise call it a payment tracker (benchmark Part 1b). NAB replaced a paid trace with in-app tracking.
      Time estimate Expected by {date} ETA, Estimated arrival Instant, Immediately, Real-time Ranges, not promises (P3).
      Time ceiling No later than {date} Within X business days — Use when only a regulatory ceiling is known. Revolut shows a max time to resolve.
      Card hold Held Authorised, Pre-auth, Pending charge Blocked Says what happened to the money. Monzo explains a pending payment as money set aside.
      Interaction Select Click, Tap, Press — One verb across surfaces.

      Banned everywhere

      These fail lint in any status string: instant, instantly, immediately, real-time, guaranteed, oops, uh oh, simply, just, soon, shortly, we appreciate your patience, any inconvenience, invalid, could not be processed, something went wrong, an error occurred.

      False-positive check: "just" is blocked only as an adverb before a verb ("just select"). "Soon" is allowed only inside agent_definition text explaining why we can't give a date.

      Message patterns and templates

      Every status message is built from the same five slots. Surfaces show more or fewer slots; they never reorder them.

      Slot Answers Max chars Required
      label What state is it in? 16 Always
      headline What happened, in their terms? 60 Always
      body Where is the money, who has it, when will it finish? 160 Not on chip or SMS
      next_step What should I do? (or "Nothing to do.") 60 Always
      cta Button: verb + object 24 When allowed_actions is not empty

      Character limits include 1.35× localisation headroom.

      Pattern by state

      SCHEDULED — Situation: Routine.

      • Headline: "{amount} to {payee} on {date}"
      • Body: "We'll take the money from {account} on {date}."
      • Next step: "Nothing to do. You can edit or cancel until {cutoff}."
      • CTA: "Edit payment" / "Cancel payment"

      ACTION_NEEDED — Situation: Blocked. Action first.

      • Headline: from cause ("Add money to pay {payee} on {date}")
      • Body: what happens if they don't act, and by when. "If you don't, this payment won't go on {date}."
      • Next step: the one action.
      • CTA: names the action ("Add money", "Confirm it's you")

      IN_PROGRESS — Situation: Waiting. Always an end condition.

      • Headline: from cause
      • Body: "Expected by {eta}." If no ETA: "No later than {max_by}." If neither: say why ("{party} doesn't share updates. Most payments arrive in {typical_range}.")
      • Delayed: add "This is taking longer than usual." plus the new end condition.
      • Next step: "Nothing to do. We'll tell you when it arrives."

      COMPLETED — Situation: Routine. Celebratory only for effortful goals the customer chose (paid off balance).

      • Headline: milestone verb by journey
      • Body: date and reference. "Completed on {date}. Reference {ref}."
      • Next step: "Nothing to do." or a receipt link

      NOT_COMPLETED — Situation: Failure. Level, specific, one apology only if we caused it.

      • Headline: what happened, from cause
      • Body: where the money is (money_moved), who stopped it, and why in their terms
      • Next step: one action, or "Nothing to do. Your money is in your account."
      • CTA: "Try again" only when a retry can work. Never for risk_block or details_wrong without a fix step.

      CHANGED_AFTER_DONE — Situation: Failure (Returned) or Routine (Refunded).

      • Headline: who changed it and the amount
      • Body: where the money is now and any fee impact
      • Next step: one action ("Check {payee}'s details and pay again") or "Nothing to do."

      Worked examples

      Case Label Headline Body Next step
      Card bill paid from external bank (CDR-0001) Completed {card} received your payment We credited {amount} on {date}. It can take a few business days to leave your bank account. Nothing to do.
      UPI transfer stuck In progress {party} has your payment Taking longer than usual. If it doesn't arrive by {max_by}, the money comes back to your account automatically. Nothing to do. We'll tell you when it's done.
      Bill payment returned Returned {party} sent your payment back Your {amount} is back in your account. Your card balance is still due on {due_date}. Pay again from another account.

      Agent fields

      Every content ID also carries fields for AI agents and support:

      • agent_definition: one-paragraph explanation of the state in plain words
      • agent_must_say: facts the agent must include (ETA, money location)
      • agent_must_not_say: claims it must never make ("It will arrive today", "The merchant is at fault")
      • escalate_if: conditions that route to a human (past max_by, dispute, fraud)
      • legal_note: regulatory timing the agent may cite, with source

      Surfaces

      One content record feeds every surface. Each surface adapter picks slots and trims; it never rewrites.

      Surface Slots shown Component Notes
      Activity list row label Status chip Chip only for non-COMPLETED rows. A completed row shows no chip; absence means done.
      Payment detail label, headline, body, next_step, cta Tracker + banner Tracker hidden when tracking_available is false.
      Home / account card headline, cta Banner Only ACTION_NEEDED, NOT_COMPLETED, delayed IN_PROGRESS. Max one at a time; most urgent wins.
      Push notification headline, short body Notification Sent on trigger only (see Product rules). Under 110 chars total.
      SMS sms field — No links in security or failure messages. "Open the {app} app" instead. Under 160 chars.
      Email headline, body, next_step, cta Email template Subject = headline.
      Statement / PDF label Text tag Statement uses the label as plain text. No colour dependence.
      AI agent / chatbot agent fields + headline, body get_payment_status tool Agent quotes headline and body, adds agent_must_say. Never improvises status.
      IVR / voice headline, next_step Voice prompt Dates spoken in full ("Tuesday, 24 November"). No reference numbers unless asked.
      Support console All fields + reason_code, rail status Agent view Shows our state next to the raw rail status so agents can explain mismatches.
      Merchant / partner API state ID, cause JSON External consumers get IDs, not copy.

      Adapter rules

      1. Resolve once. The adapter calls the content resolver with (state, cause, journey, surface, locale) and gets one record.
      2. Trim, don't rewrite. If a slot is too long for the surface, the record must carry a short variant (headline_short). Adapters never truncate mid-sentence.
      3. Fail safe. If resolution fails, show the state's default record (pay.{state}.default). Never show a raw rail status or a blank chip.
      4. Same moment, same words. Push, email and in-app for one event use the same event_id and content version.

      Components

      Tracker steps by journey

      Steps are customer milestones. They are the same for every rail in a journey. This is the Uber/Swiggy idea: the customer sees "on the way," not "clearing house batch 2."

      Journey Step 1 Step 2 Step 3 Step 4
      pay_bill Scheduled Sent Received by {payee} —
      send Sent With {party} Received —
      receive On its way Received Available —
      card_purchase Held Paid — —
      refund Started by {merchant} On its way Back in your account —
      request Requested Approved Paid —

      ACTION_NEEDED and NOT_COMPLETED show on the current step as a marker, not as a new step. The tracker never grows.

      Tech

      Status is an event stream, not a field someone edits. Copy is data, not code.

      Rules: the state machine rejects transitions not in the lifecycle. Events are idempotent on event_id. Late events with an older occurred_at are stored but don't change the shown state.

      Content IDs

      Format: pay.{state}.{cause}.{journey}, lowercase. Example: pay.in_progress.waiting_on_other_party.send.

      Fallback resolution

      The resolver tries, in order, and returns the first match:

      1. pay.{state}.{cause}.{journey}
      2. pay.{state}.{cause}
      3. pay.{state}.default.{journey}
      4. pay.{state}.default

      Level 4 must exist for all seven states. A build fails if it doesn't. This is what makes a new rail safe on day one: even with zero new copy, every state renders.

      Slot safety

      Every {slot} declares a type and a fallback. If a required slot is missing and has no fallback, the record is marked BLOCK_RENDER and the resolver drops to the next fallback level. "Hi undefined" cannot ship.

      Slot Type Fallback
      payee string "who you're paying"
      merchant string "the merchant"
      party string "their bank"
      amount money BLOCK_RENDER
      eta date range drop line; use max_by
      max_by date drop line; use typical range
      date date BLOCK_RENDER

      Monitoring

      • cause_unmapped rate per rail. Alert above 1%.
      • Time in IN_PROGRESS past max_by. Alert per payment and auto-route to support.
      • Fallback level hit rate. Level 4 above 5% means a journey is missing copy.

      Adding a new rail or payment method

      A new rail should take days of mapping, not weeks of copy. Follow these steps in order.

      1. List every raw status the rail can return, with reason codes. Source: the rail's spec, not a sales deck.
      2. Map each to a state. Use the one-test meanings in The status set. If a raw status could be two states, it is two rows split by a condition.
      3. Map each to a cause. Use existing causes. If none fits, use the default cause and file a cause request (step 7).
      4. Set modifiers. Does the rail give an ETA? A ceiling? Tracking? Can money be held? Where is the point of no return?
      5. Pick the journey. Most rails reuse send, pay_bill or receive. The tracker steps come from the journey, so the rail inherits them.
      6. Run the fallback test. Render every mapped row on every surface using only existing content. Anything that reads wrong is a gap.
      7. Close gaps in this order: new modifier value → new cause → new journey template. A new state is the last resort and needs a CDR with the pressure-test evidence.
      8. Confirm regulation. Add any timing rule to Regulatory hooks with counselConfirmed: false until signed.
      9. Ship behind monitoring. Watch cause_unmapped and fallback level 4 for two weeks.

      Definition of done

      • Every raw status maps to one state.
      • Every state renders on every surface with no BLOCK_RENDER.
      • cause_unmapped under 1% in production.
      • The support console shows the raw status next to our state.

      When rules change

      A regulation change (new TAT, new disclosure) edits the regulatory hook and the affected max_by logic or legal_note. It does not change states or labels. Content owners update only records that cite the changed rule, found through the usage index.

      Content fundamentals

      Customers should always know three things: where the payment is, when it will finish, and whether they need to do anything.

      • Seven states, fixed labels. Scheduled · Action needed · In progress · Completed · Available · Failed / Declined / Canceled / Expired · Returned / Refunded / Disputed. Detail goes in the headline, never a new label.
      • Ranges, not promises. "Expected by Thu, 20 Nov." "No later than Wed, 19 Nov." Never "instant" or "soon."
      • Say where the money is. Every not-done message does: "We didn't take any money." "Your $250.00 is back in your account."
      • Name who acts. "HDFC Bank has your payment," not "Your payment is being processed."
      • One next step. An action, or "Nothing to do."
      • Use names, not roles. "Asha R received ₹2,000," not "Payee credited."

      Executive summary

      The best players run two status layers. Underneath sits a detailed system model with 6 to 10 states. On top, customers see 4 or 5 plain labels. The gap between them is designed on purpose, not left to chance.

      1. Two layers, mapped on purpose. Wise has 10 API states, such as incoming_payment_waiting and outgoing_payment_sent. Its docs tell partners to show 8 plain labels instead, such as "On its way to Wise" (Wise docs). Stripe maps its 7 PaymentIntent states to 5 Dashboard labels (Stripe).
      2. Customer labels fall into 5 buckets. Upcoming (scheduled). In progress (pending, processing). Done (completed, posted, success). Didn't go through (failed, declined, canceled, reverted). Changed after done (refunded, returned, disputed).
      3. "Pending" is the most overloaded word in payments. It means a card hold at Monzo, a P2P payment waiting on the recipient at Cash App, and a compliance review at Revolut and PayPal. Each needs a different user action. One label hides that.
      4. "Done" is not one moment. Capital One can post a payment but hold the available credit for about 3 to 9 days (Capital One). Wise marks a transfer Complete when it reaches the recipient's bank, before it lands in their account (Wise). Leaders say which milestone was reached.
      5. Status can move backward. An ACH payment can settle, then return (Plaid). A Razorpay payment can fail, then authorize late (Razorpay). UPI has a "deemed approved" state for timeouts (PhonePe Business). The model must allow late and reverse transitions.
      6. Time is part of the status. Revolut shows the maximum time a pending card payment can take. Wise shows an arrival estimate. Regulators set outer limits: RBI's T+1 and T+5 reversal rules, Reg E's 10 and 45 days, and Reg Z's 30 and 90 days.
      7. Actions belong to states. Cancel, edit, retry, and dispute each open and close at set points. Stripe allows cancel only before processing. Capital One won't dispute a pending charge. Zelle allows cancel only while the recipient hasn't enrolled.

      Internal states vs. customer labels

      The strongest pattern: system names never reach the customer. Wise says so in its docs. Its API names differ from what the app shows, because the front end uses friendlier language (Wise). "Completed" on Wise's site means outgoing_payment_sent in the API (Wise).

      This gives four architecture rules:

      • Many-to-one mapping. Several system states collapse into one label. Stripe's requires_confirmation and processing both read as "in progress" to a customer.
      • Separate milestones. Plaid splits submitted, settled, and spendable. Card issuers split posted from available credit. Each split is a chance to tell the customer something true.
      • Reasons travel with failures. ISO 20022 requires a reason with every rejection. A label like "Failed" without a reason forces a support call.
      • Late events are normal. Returns, chargebacks, late authorizations, and deemed approvals all arrive after a "final" state. The model must accept them without breaking the story.

      Design patterns that recur

      • The step tracker. A short line of named stages, with the current one lit. Wise uses three. Best where the money passes through other parties.
      • The time promise. An estimate for the happy path, and a maximum for the slow one. Revolut and Wise both do this.
      • The "don't retry" guard. Google Pay tells users not to pay again while a UPI payment is processing (Google Pay). It prevents double debits.
      • Status-gated actions. The cancel button exists only while cancel is possible. Amex shows Cancel Payment only while a payment is "Processing" (Amex).
      • Honest milestones. Wise explains that "Complete" means sent, not arrived (Wise). The label is short, and the detail sets the expectation.
      • Quiet pending. In 2016, Monzo staff said it avoided marking pending items in the feed so users wouldn't need to think in those terms (Monzo Community). Users pushed back in the same thread. This is a real trade-off, not a settled answer.

      Anti-patterns to avoid

      • Jargon labels. A 2012 Finovate review faulted Citi for calling a card payment a "payment request" (Finovate). UK Open Banking guidance likewise says to avoid "payment initiation" and "payee" (OBL).
      • Two words for one state. NPCI's UPI Help guidelines use both "Approved" and "Success" for a successful payment (NPCI).
      • Pending with no clock. Users can't tell normal from stuck.

      Part 3: Journey map and proposed shared status model

      One model with six customer-facing states can carry every payment type in scope. Each product maps its system states into it. This is a proposal built from the benchmark, not a market standard.

      Most payments run left to right. "In progress" is highlighted because the benchmark shows most confusion lives there. The bottom row catches failures and the late events that arrive after "done."

      Journey map (text)

      1. Set up. The customer picks an amount, date, and source. Show the cut-off and the date the payment will count.
      2. Upcoming. Scheduled or autopay. Show the date and the last moment to edit or cancel.
      3. In progress. Money has left or is held. Show the cause (bank, merchant, recipient, review), an estimate, and a maximum.
      4. Done. The milestone is reached: received by the issuer, posted, or sent. Say which one.
      5. Available. Credit or funds can be used. Show this as its own step only when it lags "Done," as with payment holds.
      6. Didn't go through. Give the reason, whether money moved, when it comes back, and the next step.
      7. Changed after done. A return, refund, reversal, or dispute. Add a new entry and link it to the original.

      Part 4: One status system across every surface

      The system works by keeping three things apart: the state (what's true about the payment), the content (the approved words for that state) and the surface (how each channel shows those words). Every surface reads the same state and the same content, and no surface writes its own status copy. A visual map is on the Payment Status System Across Surfaces whiteboard.

      Layer 1: One source of status

      Every product emits one event when a payment changes state, and every surface listens to that event. Wise's API docs describe tracking transfers this way, through webhooks that fire on each state change (Wise).

      Layer 4: The AI agent reads the system

      The agent never works out a payment's status on its own. It calls a tool such as get_payment_status(payment_id), which returns the event plus the content fields for that ID. It may phrase things in its own words, within these limits:

      • Use the canonical label ("Processing"), not a synonym.
      • Never promise a time beyond max_by.
      • Offer only actions listed in allowed_actions.
      • Hand off to a human when escalate_if is met.

      This follows the safe pattern Eesel describes for banking AI: identify the intent, retrieve from an approved knowledge base, check confidence, then either answer from those sources or hand off to a human (Eesel).

      Worked example

      Event: bill payment, IN_PROGRESS, cause = issuer, eta = Thu 20 Nov. The amount is illustrative.

      • App: "Processing · Usually posts by Thu, 20 Nov"
      • Push: "Your $250 card payment is on its way."
      • Email: the same headline, plus what "posted" means and a link to the help article.
      • Agent: "Your payment is processing. It usually posts by Thursday. It's too late to cancel this one." The agent can say this because cancel isn't in allowed_actions.

      Governance

      • One owner per ID. Content design owns the words, and product owns the state logic.
      • Change once, update everywhere. Edit a field and every surface picks it up.
      • Tag by ID. Tag analytics and complaints with the content ID, so complaint data shows which exact message failed.
      • Consistency test. For each ID, every surface uses the same label and makes the same time promise.

      Where to start

      1. Pick one journey, such as card bill payments.
      2. List every status string it uses today, across the app, push, email, SMS and the bot.
      3. Map each string to a canonical state and cause. Gaps and duplicates show up quickly.
      4. Write the field set for those IDs.
      5. Pilot on three surfaces: app, push and the AI agent. The agent is the strictest test, because it exposes any missing definition.

      Design and content principles

      These come from the patterns above. Each one names its evidence.

      1. One model, many products. Map every product's system states into the same canonical set. Wise and Stripe both separate system names from what customers see.
      2. Name the milestone, not the mechanism. Say "Payment received," not "ACSC." Say what happened to the customer's money.
      3. Split "pending" by cause. Waiting on a merchant, a recipient, a review, or the network are different stories with different actions.
      4. Every in-progress state carries a clock. Give an estimate and a maximum. Revolut and Wise prove it works.
      5. Every failure carries a reason and a next step. ISO 20022 requires reasons on rejection. Monzo surfaces decline reasons in the app.
      6. Actions follow state. Show cancel, edit, retry, or dispute only when they'll work. Hide them the moment they won't.
      7. Plan for the story to change. Returns, reversals, late approvals, and disputes arrive after "done." Write content for each one in advance.
      8. Same words on every surface. The list, detail screen, alert, and help article must use the same label for the same state.
      9. Let regulation set the outer promise. Reg Z, Reg E, and RBI's TAT rules give hard time limits. Use them as the maximum, never the estimate.

      Adyen, in plain English

      Adyen lists about 60 statuses, but most are accounting variants. Read the page as five stages:

      1. Offer: the shopper is still paying (Received, Open).
      2. Authorised: the bank said yes and the money is reserved. The merchant can still cancel or capture.
      3. Settling: SentForSettle means capture was requested and can no longer be cancelled. Settled means Adyen has the money.
      4. Refund: SentForRefund, then Refunded. The shopper may wait up to 40 days.
      5. Dispute: Chargeback, then ChargebackReversed if the merchant wins, or SecondChargeback, which is final.

      Failures sit beside these stages: Refused, Error, Cancelled, and Expired (authorised but never captured, usually after 28 days for cards).

      What to skip: statuses ending in Externally, Bulk, Installment or Scheduled, plus the Payout and Stored value groups. They matter for accounting, not for status design.

      Why it matters for us: at every stage, Adyen splits "request sent" from "outcome confirmed" (SentForSettle → Settled, SentForRefund → Refunded). That's the same split as "Processing" versus "Payment received."

      NPCI links that open in a browser

      The NPCI circulars are PDFs, which may not open for everyone. These web pages cover the same points:

      • RBI turnaround-time circular: reversal deadlines and ₹100-a-day compensation, paid without waiting for a complaint.
      • NPCI UPI dispute redressal: customers can raise complaints in the app, and apps must keep them informed by updating the status.

      Reading NPCI's UPI Help guidelines in full turned up one detail: they use both "Approved" and "Success" for the same state. That's a small, real lesson in label consistency.

      UK Open Banking appendices (now read)

      • Status codes by stage. Consent (AWAU, AUTH, RJCT, COND), initiation (RCVD, PDNG, CANC, ACTC, PATC, ACCP, ACFC, ACSP), processing (ACWC, ACSC) and execution (BLCK, ACWP, ACCC, RJCT). The terminal states are CANC, RJCT, BLCK, ACWP and ACCC (OBL). There are no label rules for customers. Providers are asked to use the status to judge how confident they can be, then inform the customer.
      • Language. Use "Pay," "Transfer" and "To." Avoid "payment initiation," "set up a payment" and "payee." Make the point of no return clear (OBL).
      • Errors. Each error pairs a status with a coded reason, such as "[U036] Authorisation not completed" (OBL).

      Status label guidance (GOV.UK)

      Use adjectives, not verbs. Start with the smallest set of statuses that works. Never rely on colour alone, and keep each status's colour the same everywhere (GOV.UK).
